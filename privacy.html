Stacks

Stacks is an iPhone app that finds photos taken moments apart, picks the best photo and, when you choose, turns a moment into an Apple Photos burst. It is designed to do all of its work on your iPhone. Stacks doesn’t collect any data: nothing it reads or works out about your photos is sent to us or to anyone else.

Access to your photo library

Stacks asks for access to your photo library when you continue past its welcome screen. It is the only permission Stacks asks for. It reads your photos and their details, such as the date and time each was taken, its location, whether it’s a favorite, which of your albums it’s in, and its caption, keywords and rating. It uses them to find moments and to carry those details over when you stack photos.

You can choose Full Access or Limited Access, and change or remove access at any time in the Settings app under Privacy & Security → Photos. With Limited Access, Stacks only sees the photos you’ve selected.

Analysis on your iPhone

To find moments and pick the best photo, Stacks uses Apple’s Vision and Core Image frameworks, which run on your iPhone. For each photo it considers, it works out a numeric “fingerprint” of the image (used to tell whether two photos look alike), Apple’s rating of the photo’s overall quality, how sharp it is (overall and on its main subject), whether it looks hazy as through a smudged lens, whether a cat or dog in it is facing the camera, and a 16 × 16-pixel color thumbnail used to match a blurry photo to the sharp ones around it.

Stacks also checks what each photo shows: whether there are people in it, found by their bodies so that someone facing away or far off still counts; whether there’s a cat or dog in it, looking at the camera or not; and whether Apple’s image labels see a document, receipt, whiteboard or card, and where the page is. To tell the pages of a document apart, Stacks reads the text in a photo, on your iPhone. Stacks keeps only scrambled codes (hashes) of the words and a number for how readable the text is, never the text itself.

Scanning uses the copy of each photo that is already on your iPhone and never downloads photos from iCloud. The one exception is High Quality Scan, which runs only when you start it: High Quality Scan asks iOS to download a sharper copy of each moment’s key photo that’s kept only in iCloud, after showing about how much it will download, and checks that copy the same way.

Face analysis

Many of the photos Stacks compares show people, so it also analyzes faces, on your iPhone, with Apple’s Vision and Core Image frameworks. For each face it detects, Stacks records where the face is in the photo, the angle of the head, an estimate of the face’s image quality, whether the person appears to be smiling, how open their eyes are, and a numeric face fingerprint.

Stacks uses this only to group and rank photos: for example, to notice that someone blinked or looked away, or that a new person joined the group. It doesn’t try to identify who anyone is, and it doesn’t know or use names. Face data never leaves your iPhone and is never shared. A feedback report you choose to share, described below, includes only how many faces Stacks found. It is stored only with the rest of the analysis described below, which is left out of backups and deleted when you delete the app.

What Stacks stores on your iPhone

Stacks keeps the following in its private storage on your iPhone. None of it is sent to us.

  • Analysis. The measurements described above, including face data and the small thumbnails, plus a note of which photos are kept only in iCloud, so Stacks doesn’t have to redo its work every time you open it. These files are excluded from iCloud and computer backups. If you restore your iPhone or move to a new one, Stacks simply works them out again.
  • A record of your stacks. So that each stack can be checked and undone, Stacks records, for every photo you stack, its identifier in your photo library, its original file name, date, location, favorite status, caption, keywords, rating, albums and Live Photo setting, along with checksums of the files and the details needed to restore them exactly. It also notes which photos you marked to delete with the stack. This record is included in your iPhone’s backups (iCloud Backup or a backup on your computer, if you use them), so you can still undo your stacks after restoring your iPhone. Those backups are made and stored by Apple or on your own computer, under your settings, and we can’t access them.
  • Settings and choices. Your settings, which photos you left out, which photo you picked as the key photo, your swipes in a moment you haven’t finished, which tips you’ve already seen, and all-time totals of photos deleted (with the space they took up) and moments stacked. These are kept in the app’s own storage on your iPhone, which is included in backups.
  • Feedback reports. If you turn on Developer Mode in Stacks’ Settings and report a moment Stacks got wrong, Stacks saves the report on your iPhone, left out of backups. For each photo in that moment, a report lists the photo’s identifier in your photo library, the date and time it was taken, and Stacks’ measurements, such as its score and how many faces, people and pets Stacks found. Reports contain no photos, no text from them and no locations. A report leaves your iPhone only if you share it from Settings → Feedback Reports.
  • Temporary copies. While stacking or unstacking, Stacks briefly writes copies of the photos involved to its temporary storage and removes them as soon as the step finishes.

Changes to your photo library

Scanning only reads your library. Stacks changes your library only when you ask it to. When you stack a moment, it adds copies of the photos, tagged so Photos shows them as one burst, and moves the key photo’s date in Photos to just before the first photo so it becomes the cover. The time recorded in the photo file itself is unchanged. Stacks then asks iOS to remove the separate originals. If a copy ever fails its check, Stacks asks iOS to delete the copies it just made, and your originals are left as they were.

Every deletion, whether from stacking, unstacking, Clean Up or Keep Best Photo Only, needs your approval in iOS’s own confirmation, and deleted photos stay in Recently Deleted for 30 days.

Network connections and iCloud

Stacks makes no network connections of its own. It contains no networking code, and we check every release build to confirm that.

If you use iCloud Photos with Optimize iPhone Storage, some of your originals are kept in iCloud. When Stacks needs one, to show a sharper copy of a photo you’re looking at, to stack a photo whose original is only in iCloud, or during a High Quality Scan you’ve started, Stacks asks iOS, and iOS downloads the photo from Apple’s servers as it would for the Photos app.

When you share a new stack’s key photo, Stacks hands a copy to iOS’s share sheet with the photo’s location removed, and you choose where it goes.

What Stacks doesn’t do

  • No account or sign-in.
  • No analytics, crash-reporting or advertising code, whether ours or another company’s.
  • No tracking across apps or websites, and no use of the advertising identifier.
  • No other permissions. Stacks doesn’t ask for your camera, microphone, contacts, location services, health data or notifications. It reads the location already saved in your photos, never your iPhone’s current location.

Your choices

  • Change or remove Stacks’ access to your photos at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Photos.
  • Delete Stacks to remove everything it stored on your iPhone: its analysis, its record of your stacks and its settings. Copies of the record and settings in existing backups remain until those backups are replaced or deleted.
  • Deleting Stacks doesn’t remove your photos. Stacks you made stay in Apple Photos as ordinary bursts. Unstack anything you want as separate photos before deleting the app, because without its record Stacks can’t undo them later.

Information handled by Apple

Our apps are distributed through Apple’s App Store and TestFlight. Apple handles the following under its own privacy policy, at apple.com/privacy.

  • Downloads and purchases. Apple processes App Store downloads and any purchases, and never shares your payment details with us. We receive sales and download reports that don’t identify you.
  • Crash reports and app analytics. If you’ve chosen to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone’s settings, Apple may share crash reports and usage statistics with us. They don’t identify you, and you can turn sharing off at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements.
  • TestFlight. If you test a pre-release version of one of our apps, Apple shares with us the feedback and screenshots you choose to send, crash reports, and basic information such as your device model, iOS version and how often you open the app. If we invited you by email, we also have your name and email address. If you joined through a public link, Apple doesn’t share your name or email address with us. We use this only to improve the app.
